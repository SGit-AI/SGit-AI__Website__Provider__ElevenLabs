OpenRouter — the sibling page, as a structural stub

This is not an OpenRouter site. It is a deliberately short stub of the sibling provider page, published here for two reasons: it supplies the other rows of the comparison, and it is the proof that adding a provider to this family is one Markdown file with front-matter rather than template surgery.

Prose from the video vault at commit 7d1916aca5f3, 7 September 2026. Condensed from platforms/openrouter.md. The full page belongs at openrouter.providers.sgit.ai, which does not exist yet. When the vault moves ahead, this page is behind — and says so rather than guessing.

Scope. This site reports on ElevenLabs. The full OpenRouter report — nine sections, its own cost table, its own failures — belongs at openrouter.providers.sgit.ai when that site exists. What follows is the minimum needed to make the comparison honest, plus the one measurement that is genuinely evidence for the whole argument.

Why it is the useful contrast

OpenRouter can mint a key with a spend limit and a reset window, and reports limit, usage and limit_remaining against it. So a key in a page has a blast radius somebody chose in advance. ElevenLabs text to speech has no equivalent at any price vendor docs 5 Sep 2026 — which is the single sharpest difference between the two and the reason the comparison table earns its place.

What went wrong

  • The limit did its job — a 402 at $4.79 of $5.00, because audio output requires $0.50 of headroom. The full framing is on the patterns page, where it belongs: it is the one piece of evidence on this site that a bound is enforceable in practice rather than in a diagram verified 3 Sep 2026.
  • No speed control. The model takes no pace parameter, so a portrait cut that had to fit under a three-minute limit lost a third of its script instead of 15% of its pace measured 2 Sep 2026. That single missing knob is what sent us to evaluate a vendor whose API has one vendor docs 5 Sep 2026.
  • An outro that lied. A reel's closing narration said "no API cost" over a slide printing $0.1554, because one outro served both a free cut and a paid one. Caught by reading the closing frame; the rule now is that every outro says "the cost is on the screen".
